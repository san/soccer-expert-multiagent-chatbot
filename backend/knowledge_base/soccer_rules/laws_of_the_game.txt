## Overview of the Laws of the Game

The Laws of the Game are the rules by which soccer (association football) is played. They are maintained by the International Football Association Board (IFAB) and updated annually.

Total Laws: 17 main laws covering all aspects of the game

## Law 1: The Field of Play

### Dimensions
- Length (touchline): 100-130 yards (90-120 meters)
- Width (goal line): 50-100 yards (45-90 meters)
- Professional standard: 115 yards × 74 yards (105m × 68m)

### Field Markings
- Touchline: Long side boundaries
- Goal line: Short side boundaries at goals
- Halfway line: Divides field into two halves
- Center circle: 10-yard radius around center spot
- Center spot: Where kick-off occurs
- Goal area: 6 yards × 20 yards (penalty box extension)
- Penalty area: 18 yards × 44 yards
- Penalty spot: 12 yards from goal line
- Penalty arc: 10-yard arc outside penalty area

### Goal
- Width: 8 yards (7.32 meters)
- Height: 8 feet (2.44 meters)
- Goal lines and posts marked with white lines
- Net attached behind goal

## Law 2: The Ball

### Specifications
- Circumference: 27-28 inches (68-70 centimeters)
- Weight: 14-16 ounces (410-450 grams)
- Air pressure: 0.6-1.1 atmosphere at sea level
- Material: Typically synthetic leather or rubber
- Spherical in shape

### Ball In/Out of Play
- Ball in play: When entire ball is not over goal line or touchline
- Ball out of play: When entire ball crosses goal line or touchline (in air or on ground)
- Also out when play stopped by referee

## Law 3: The Players

### Number of Players
- Each team: 11 players maximum on field
- Typically: 1 goalkeeper, 10 outfield players
- Minimum: 7 players to continue match (rules vary by league)

### Goalkeeper
- Designated goalkeeper wears different colored jersey
- Only player allowed to handle ball with hands in penalty area
- Can use hands for:
  - Catching the ball
  - Throwing the ball
  - Rolling the ball with hands or feet
- Cannot handle:
  - Ball deliberately kicked to them by teammate
  - Ball thrown directly in by teammate

### Substitutions
- Each team allowed: typically 3 substitutions (up to 5 in some competitions)
- Substitutes must enter at halfway line when beckoned by referee
- Substituted player cannot return to field

## Law 4: Players' Equipment

### Required Equipment
- Jersey/shirt
- Shorts (darker than jersey if possible)
- Socks (covering shin guard)
- Shin guards (protective equipment under socks)
- Appropriate footwear (boots or shoes with studs)
- Goalkeeper gloves (not required but standard)

### Equipment Colors
- Each team wears contrasting colors
- Goalkeeper wears color different from both teams
- Numbers on back (typically 1-99)
- Optional: Name on back

### Safety Requirements
- No jewelry (rings, necklaces, earrings)
- No items that endanger other players
- Shin guards mandatory (FIFA rule)
- Any equipment injury risks checked by referee

## Law 5: The Referee

### Authority
- Sole judge of fact
- Enforces Laws of the Game
- Main official with full authority
- Keeps official time
- Signals start/end of periods

### Referee Powers
- Issues warnings (yellow cards)
- Sends off players (red cards)
- Stops play for injuries
- Restarts play with referee's whistle
- Determines goals and no-goals (VAR assistance allowed)
- Full control over game conduct

### Referee Equipment
- Whistle (to signal decisions)
- Yellow cards (warnings/cautions)
- Red cards (ejection)
- Notebook (recording incidents)
- Timepiece (tracking match time)

## Law 6: The Other Match Officials

### Assistant Referees (Linesmen)
- Two assistants on sidelines
- Flag signals for:
  - Offside decisions
  - Throw-ins
  - Corner kicks vs. goal kicks
  - Out of bounds
- Flag held horizontally (throw-in), diagonal (offside), straight up (goal kick), etc.

### Fourth Official
- Manages substitutions
- Displays substitution numbers on board
- Assists with timekeeping
- Backs up main referee

### Video Assistant Referee (VAR)
- Introduced at World Cup 2018
- Reviews contentious decisions:
  - Goals and whether ball fully crossed line
  - Penalty decisions
  - Red card incidents
  - Case of mistaken identity
- Can recommend on-field review

## Law 7: Match Duration

### Standard Match
- Duration: 90 minutes
- Two halves: 45 minutes each
- Half-time break: 15 minutes (typically)

### Stoppage Time
- Referee adds extra time for stoppages:
  - Injuries
  - Substitutions
  - Time-wasting
  - Deliberate delays
- Typically 1-5 minutes per half (discretionary)

### Draws and Extra Time
- Group stage: Match ends in draw (no extra time)
- Knockout matches: Extra time of 2 × 15 minutes
- If still tied after extra time: Penalty shootout

## Law 8: Start and Restart of Play

### Kick-Off
- Occurs at center spot with ball at rest
- All players except opposing two outside center circle
- Ball kicked forward (toward opposing goal)
- Kicker cannot touch ball again until another player touches it
- Goal cannot be scored directly from kick-off

### Restart After Stoppage
- Referee signals restart with whistle
- Various restart methods:
  - Kick-off (after goal, half-time, delay)
  - Throw-in (from touchline)
  - Corner kick (when defending team puts ball over goal line)
  - Goal kick (when attacking team puts ball over goal line)
  - Free kick (after foul)
  - Penalty kick (for foul in penalty area)

### Dropped Ball (Restart)
- Used when referee stops play for external reason
- Referee drops ball between two opposing players
- Ball allowed to touch ground before play resumes

## Law 9: The Ball In and Out of Play

### Ball In Play
- When entire ball is between goal lines and touchlines (including in air)
- Remains in play if:
  - Touches referee or assistant
  - Bounces off goal post, crossbar
  - Remains in air inside field boundaries

### Ball Out of Play
- When entire ball crosses goal line or touchline
- When play stopped by referee
- Restarted per Laws 8-11 (various restart methods)

## Law 10: Determining the Outcome

### Scoring a Goal
- Entire ball must cross goal line between goal posts and under crossbar
- Ball can be any size ball in play
- Ball may be intentionally or unintentionally directed
- Goal cannot be disallowed for hand ball by attacking player (unless hand-to-ball direct contact deliberately)

### Goal Scoring Rules
- Player may score from:
  - Direct kick
  - Rebound off goal post or crossbar
  - From own header, chest, any body part (except deliberate hand)
  - Deflections off opponents or own players

### Winning Match
- Team scoring most goals wins
- If tied: 
  - Group stage: Draw (each team gets 1 point)
  - Knockout stage: Extra time, then penalties

### Golden Goal/Silver Goal
- Phased out in modern soccer
- Previously: Sudden-death extra time or first goal in extra time
- Current: Standard extra time then penalties

## Law 11: Offside

### Offside Position
- Player is in offside position when:
  - Player is nearer to opponent's goal line than both ball and at least 2 opponents
  - Typically when ahead of last defender and goalkeeper
  - OR nearer to goal line than last defensive player

### Offside NOT Called When
- Player is in own half of field
- Player level with last 2 opponents
- Player level with ball
- Player receives ball directly from:
  - Throw-in
  - Goal kick
  - Corner kick

### Offside Offense
- Offside position alone is not an offense
- Offense when player:
  - Actively participates in play
  - Interferes with opponent
  - Gains advantage from offside position
  - Ball passed back by teammate (typical offside scenario)

### Penalty for Offside
- Indirect free kick to defending team from offside position

### Recent Offside Technology
- Goal-line technology confirms goals
- VAR reviews offside decisions
- Semi-automated offside systems being tested (2024+)

## Law 12: Fouls and Misconduct

### Direct Free Kick Offenses (Direct Free Kick Awarded)
- Punching/striking opponent with hand
- Kicking/attempting to kick opponent
- Jumping at opponent
- Charging opponent violently or dangerously
- Pushing opponent
- Tackling opponent dangerously
- Spitting at opponent
- Deliberately handling ball (except goalkeeper in penalty area)
- Deliberately denying goal-scoring opportunity by hand
- Dangerous play (overhead kicks near opponent)

### Indirect Free Kick Offenses
- Dangerous play without contact
- Obstruction of opponent
- Goalkeeper handling deliberate back-pass from teammate
- Goalkeeper handling throw-in from teammate
- Unsporting behavior
- Excessive celebration
- Time-wasting
- Wearing inappropriate equipment
- Entering/re-entering field without permission
- Leaving field without permission

### Penalty Kick
- Awarded for direct free kick offenses in penalty area
- Taken from penalty spot (12 yards from goal)
- All players except kicker and goalkeeper must be outside penalty area
- If goal not scored, can restart normally

## Law 13: Free Kicks

### Direct Free Kick
- Goal can be scored directly
- Awarded for contact fouls and handball

### Indirect Free Kick
- Goal cannot be scored directly
- Must be touched by second player before scoring
- Marked by referee raising arm overhead

### Free Kick Procedure
- Ball placed at offense location
- Referee signals when all players positioned
- Kicker strikes ball (ball must move to be in play)
- Cannot touch ball again until another player touches it
- Ball placed on ground (not in hand)

## Law 14: Penalty Kick

### When Awarded
- Direct free kick offense by defending team in their penalty area
- Intentional handball in penalty area
- Deliberately preventing goal-scoring chance by handball

### Penalty Kick Procedure
- Ball placed on penalty spot
- Goalkeeper on goal line between posts
- Goalkeeper cannot move feet until kick taken
- All other players outside penalty area and penalty arc
- Kicker approaches and strikes ball
- Goal scored if ball enters goal and doesn't go out of play

### Penalty Kick Outcomes
- Goal: If ball fully crosses goal line
- No goal: If ball goes wide, over, or hits post/crossbar
- Retake: If goalkeeper moved illegally (foot movement before kick)
- Penalty to other team: If kicker offended (e.g., kicked ball twice)

## Law 15: The Throw-In

### When Awarded
- Ball goes out of play over touchline
- Awarded to team that didn't last touch ball
- Taken from point ball went out

### Throw-In Procedure
- Thrower faces field with both feet on or behind touchline
- Both hands used, ball thrown from behind and over head
- Both feet must remain on ground during throw
- Ball must enter field from above head level
- Thrower cannot touch ball again until another player touches it

### Throw-In Outcomes
- Goal cannot be scored directly from throw-in
- If improperly thrown: Throw-in to opposing team

## Law 16: Goal Kick

### When Awarded
- Attacking team last touches ball over goal line
- Ball goes out of play over goal line (not goal)

### Goal Kick Procedure
- Kicked from goal area
- Goalkeeper typically takes kick
- Ball must leave penalty area before play resumes
- Cannot score directly from goal kick
- Kicker cannot touch ball again until another player touches it

### Goal Kick Outcomes
- Goal not allowed directly
- If ball doesn't leave penalty area: Retaken
- If opponent enters penalty area: Retaken

## Law 17: The Corner Kick

### When Awarded
- Defending team last touches ball before going over goal line
- Ball doesn't go directly into goal

### Corner Kick Procedure
- Taken from corner area (1-yard arc)
- Kicked by attacking team
- Defenders must be at least 10 yards away
- Ball must be struck with foot
- Kicker cannot touch ball again until another player touches it

### Corner Kick Outcomes
- Goal can be scored directly from corner
- Most common set-play for scoring
- Often used for head-ball attempts (crosses to tall players)

## Summary of Key Rules

### Restarts
1. Kick-off: Start of half or after goal
2. Throw-in: Ball over touchline
3. Goal kick: Attacking team knocks over goal line
4. Corner kick: Defending team knocks over goal line
5. Direct free kick: Contact fouls
6. Indirect free kick: Non-contact fouls
7. Penalty kick: Direct foul in penalty area

### Misconduct
- Yellow card: Caution for misconduct
- Red card: Dismissal for severe misconduct
- Two yellows in match = one red

### Common Offenses
- Offside: Attacking near goal line with no defender
- Handball: Deliberate hand contact with ball (outside goalkeeper penalty area)
- Fouls: Contact offenses (kicking, pushing, charging)
- Dangerous play: Risk to opponent safety
